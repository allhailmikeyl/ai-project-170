TL;DR

We ask whether a city’s monthly weather helps predict if its retail rice price will go up or down next month. We implement kNN, Gaussian Naive Bayes, and a perceptron from scratch, and compare each with price-only features against price plus weather.

Dataset

We combine two public datasets, joined by city and month for 2020 to 2023.

DatasetSourceWhat it contains
Retail prices of rice PSA OpenSTAT (Cereals retail price table, 2018-based) Monthly retail rice prices per rice variety and area
Philippine Cities Weather Data (2020-2023) Kaggle (daily_data_combined_2020_to_2023.csv) Daily weather records for 137 cities, aggregated to monthly values

Target: whether next month’s retail rice price goes up or down compared with the current month.

Features: a rice variety’s recent price history, plus the city’s monthly weather in the weather-augmented setting.

Methods

All three methods are implemented from scratch, and each is run twice: with price-only features and with price plus weather features.

k-Nearest Neighbors

Classifies a city-month by the majority label among its k closest records in feature space, using a distance measure such as Euclidean distance.

Gaussian Naive Bayes

Applies Bayes’ rule with the assumption that features are independent given the class, modeling each continuous feature with a per-class Gaussian.

Perceptron

A linear classifier whose weights are updated on every misclassified example until it separates up from down, or training stops.
